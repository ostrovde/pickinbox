The five email marketing platforms worth shortlisting

There are dozens of platforms and most of them will do the job. These five come up again and again for a reason: each one is genuinely best-in-class for a specific kind of sender. The useful question is not “which is best” but “which one stops being a compromise first”.

How to read that table

Two of these are generalists (GetResponse, MailerLite), two are specialists (Kit for creators, Omnisend for e-commerce), and one is a veteran whose strength is predictability (AWeber). Almost every bad platform decision comes from picking a specialist for a generalist job, or the reverse.

If your money comes from an audience

Newsletters, courses, memberships, paid communities. Your critical flows are welcome, onboarding and re-engagement, and your landing pages matter as much as your emails. Kit is designed around exactly this, which is why it feels lighter than the others — and why it feels limited the moment you need inventory-aware messages.

If your money comes from a store

Your critical flows are abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase and win-back, and they need product data. Omnisend is built for this. A generalist can approximate it, but you will spend your time wiring integrations instead of writing emails.

If you are a small business with no clear shape yet

You want one bill for forms, landing pages, email and (sometimes) webinars, and you want to grow into automation rather than outgrow the tool. That is the generalist case: GetResponse if you will use the breadth, MailerLite if you will not.

The three questions that decide it

  1. What counts as a contact? Ask explicitly. If unsubscribed and inactive profiles are billable, your cost curve is steeper than the pricing page suggests.
  2. How many automations do I actually need? Count the flows you will build in the next 12 months. If the answer is more than two, check which tier unlocks visual automation.
  3. Can I authenticate my sending domain? SPF, DKIM and DMARC support is non-negotiable. If a platform makes this hard, it will cost you inbox placement — and placement is the whole product.

What we would not do

  • Choose on the discount. A cheap year on the wrong platform costs more than the migration you will pay for later.
  • Choose on template count. You will use three templates, not three hundred.
  • Migrate on a hunch. Import a real segment, build one flow, send one campaign, and compare open and click rates before moving the whole list.
