Kit (ex ConvertKit) review: honest notes for creators

Kit is the platform most often recommended to people who write for a living, and that reputation is mostly deserved. It is also the platform most often chosen for the wrong reason. Here is the split.

What Kit is actually good at

Publishing rhythm

The whole interface assumes you send regularly to readers. Broadcasts, sequences and subscriber tags are arranged around that workflow instead of around campaign assembly. If you publish weekly, you will feel the difference immediately: fewer clicks between “I wrote something” and “it went out”.

Starting from zero

The free tier is unusually usable for building and sending to a small list, with landing pages and forms included. For a first list of a few hundred subscribers that is a genuinely low-risk way to begin, and it is why Kit is so often the first recommendation.

Creator-economics pricing

Pricing is subscriber-first, which aligns the cost with the audience you actually monetise. Compare that against platforms where unsubscribed or long-inactive profiles remain billable — the gap widens as the list ages.

Sequences that are hard to get wrong

The automation builder is deliberately limited. If you want five conditional branches with scoring, you will be frustrated. If you want a welcome series and a lead-magnet delivery that will not misfire, the constraint is a feature.

Where Kit is the wrong tool

  • You sell physical products. Cart recovery, inventory-aware content and product recommendations are not its strength. Choose an e-commerce platform instead.
  • You need complex funnel logic. Scoring, multi-goal routing and deep time-based branching will push you toward a marketing-automation platform or an SMB suite.
  • You need transactional or heavily templated mail. Order confirmations and complex branded transactional templates are best handled by a dedicated transactional service.

Before you migrate a list into it

  1. Check the billing unit so you know what happens to inactive contacts as the list grows.
  2. Confirm custom-domain sending (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) is available on the tier you will pay for — this is the check that protects deliverability.
  3. Map your tags first. Kit is tag-driven; a messy tag structure imported from another tool will follow you for years.
  4. Rebuild, do not export automations. Budget a full day per complex sequence.
  5. Send one real campaign before cutting over. Compare open and click rates on a segment rather than moving the whole list on faith.

Who should not read this review

If you are running a store, or if you need a single subscription covering landing pages, webinars and e-commerce flows, Kit is not the shortlist entry you want — start with the platform comparison instead. If you are weighing it directly against a broader small-business suite, read Kit vs GetResponse.

Verdict

For a writer, newsletter author or course seller whose revenue is tied to the audience, Kit is the tool that gets out of the way fastest and the one we would start with. For a product business, it is the wrong shape — and no amount of discount changes that.
