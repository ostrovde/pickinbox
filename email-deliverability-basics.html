Email deliverability: the basics that decide everything

You can write a perfect email and still lose — if it never reaches the inbox. The factors that decide placement are mostly technical, and they are set before you hit send.

The three DNS records that matter

SPF — who is allowed to send as you

A DNS record listing the servers authorised to send mail for your domain. If your email platform is not listed, receivers can treat your mail as forged. Most platforms document the exact record to add; the failure mode is having two SPF records, which invalidates both.

DKIM — proof the message was not altered

A cryptographic signature applied to each message and verified against a public key in your DNS. It survives forwarding better than SPF and is the record most often missing. Your platform should give you the key and the selector name.

DMARC — what to do when the first two fail

A policy record that tells receivers how to handle mail that fails SPF or DKIM, plus where to send reports. Start with a monitoring policy that only reports, watch the reports for a few weeks, then tighten. Jumping straight to a strict policy before you know all legitimate senders will break real mail.

Shared IP or dedicated IP

On a shared pool your reputation depends partly on other senders. That is usually fine — and it is the right default for most lists, especially under a few tens of thousands of messages a month, because you inherit a warm reputation instead of building one from nothing.

A dedicated IP only makes sense if you send high volume consistently. A dedicated IP with low, irregular volume looks worse than a shared one: you never accumulate the engagement signals receivers want.

List hygiene beats clever subject lines

  • Use confirmed opt-in (double opt-in) where you can. It reduces list size and improves every downstream metric.
  • Suppress hard bounces permanently. Continuing to send to dead addresses is one of the fastest ways to damage reputation.
  • Re-engage before you drop. Run a win-back campaign on contacts who have not opened in six months; remove whoever does not respond.
  • Never send to a purchased or scraped list. No amount of technical setup survives that.

Content signals receivers actually read

  • A clear text version alongside HTML.
  • Real, working unsubscribe links that take effect immediately.
  • Consistent sending volume — sudden spikes look like a compromise.
  • Low complaint rates; make unsubscribing easier than marking spam.

What to check before you choose a platform

Custom sending domain, SPF/DKIM/DMARC support and suppression controls are non-negotiable. If a platform makes these hard, no editor feature compensates. This is check three in the ten-minute guide, and it is the check most people skip.
