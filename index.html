What actually separates them

1. How you get billed

Every platform bills on contact count, but the definition of “contact” differs — some count only subscribers, others count everyone on the list including unsubscribed and non-engaged profiles. That single difference can double your bill as the list grows.

2. Automation ceilings

Free and entry tiers usually allow one or two automations. Real lifecycle flows (welcome, onboarding, win-back, cart recovery) need a mid tier. If your plan requires automation from day one, budget for the mid tier from the start instead of migrating later.

3. Deliverability, not features

The biggest practical risk is not the editor — it is landing in the inbox. Shared IP pools, sender authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) and list hygiene matter more than template count. A platform that forces proper authentication is worth more than one with a fancier builder.

4. Migration cost

Exporting subscribers is easy; exporting automations, tags and history usually is not. Assume that switching later costs you a rebuild of every flow — so choose by where you will be in two years, not by this month’s discount.

How to choose in ten minutes

  • Write down the job: newsletters, course launches, or product sales. Those three lead to different tools.
  • Check the billing unit: does the platform count unsubscribed and inactive contacts?
  • Confirm the automation tier: how many flows do you need, and on which plan are they included?
  • Verify authentication: can you set SPF, DKIM and a custom sending domain? If not, move on.
  • Test with a real list: import 200–500 contacts, build one flow, send one campaign, watch open/click and spam placement.
  • Only then compare price. The cheapest tool you have to migrate away from is the most expensive one.
