How to choose an email marketing platform in ten minutes

Most platform comparisons start with features and end with a discount code. This one starts with four checks that eliminate most options before price is even relevant.

The four checks
  • 1. The billing unit
  • 2. The automation tier
  • 3. Sender authentication
  • 4. The cost of leaving

1. Find out what counts as a contact

Every platform bills on list size, but “list size” is defined by the vendor. Three definitions exist in practice:

  • Active subscribers only — unsubscribed and bounced profiles are free. Cheapest curve.
  • All stored profiles — including unsubscribed and long-inactive contacts. Your bill keeps growing even as engagement falls.
  • Tiered by feature, not audience — a flat-ish price that unlocks more as you pay more.

This single difference is why two platforms that look identically priced can differ by a factor of two at 20,000 contacts. Write the vendor’s definition down before you compare anything else.

2. Count the automations you will actually build

Do not ask “does it have automation” — every platform does. Ask which tier includes it, and how many flows you get. Then count honestly what you will build in the next year:

  • welcome / onboarding sequence
  • lead magnet delivery
  • re-engagement or win-back
  • abandoned cart (if you sell products)
  • post-purchase follow-up
  • segmentation-driven campaigns

If that list is longer than two, you need the visual automation tier from day one. Choosing the cheap tier and migrating in six months is the most expensive path available.

3. Confirm you can authenticate your sending domain

Deliverability is not a feature you can buy later. Before committing, verify the platform supports all three of these with your own domain:

  • SPF — authorises the platform to send on your behalf.
  • DKIM — cryptographically signs your mail so it cannot be altered in transit.
  • DMARC — tells receivers what to do when the first two fail, and gives you reporting.

If a platform cannot use your domain for sending, or makes this hard, stop the evaluation there. A beautiful editor attached to a shared sending domain is a liability.

4. Price out leaving before you arrive

Subscribers export easily. What does not export cleanly:

  • automations and their branching logic (you rebuild them by hand)
  • tags and custom fields (mapping is manual and error-prone)
  • sending reputation on a shared IP (partially resets with the new provider)
  • historical performance data you use for decisions

Assume a full day of work per complex flow. If you have six flows and a 15,000-contact list, a “cheaper” platform can easily cost you more in a year than the platform you were avoiding.

The ten-minute procedure

  1. Write your shape in one line: newsletter, course, or store.
  2. Look up the billing unit for two candidates (active vs all profiles).
  3. Check which tier includes visual automation, and how many flows.
  4. Confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC with your own domain.
  5. Import a real 200–500 contact segment and build one flow.
  6. Send a campaign and compare open, click and spam placement.
  7. Only now compare price — and compare it at two years of expected list growth, not today.
