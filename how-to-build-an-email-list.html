How to build an email list from zero

Building a list is not a growth hack. It is a trade: you give something genuinely useful now, in exchange for permission to be useful later. Everything below follows from that.

1. Decide what you are trading

Pick one offer, not three. A single specific thing works far better than a generic “subscribe for updates”:

  • a short practical guide on one narrow problem
  • a checklist or template that saves an hour of work
  • a small tool or calculator
  • a mini-course delivered over 3–5 emails

Write the promise in one sentence. If you cannot, the offer is too vague to convert.

2. Put the form where the intent already is

Order of effectiveness, from strongest to weakest:

  1. Inside the content — a form or link at the point where a reader has just got value.
  2. End of each article — a single specific call to action, matching that article’s topic.
  3. A dedicated landing page — one page, one promise, one field.
  4. Site-wide footer or popup — weakest intent; use sparingly and never on first load.

Ask for the minimum. Every extra field costs conversions, and you can collect the rest later through conversation.

3. Make the first three emails automatic

Build a short welcome sequence before you promote anything:

  • Email 1 — deliver. The thing you promised, immediately, with no upsell.
  • Email 2 — context. Why the problem matters, and the most common mistake people make.
  • Email 3 — next step. One clear action: read this, try this, reply with your situation.

This sequence is the reason people stay. Most lists die in the first week because nothing arrives.

4. Send on a schedule you can hold for a year

Weekly is the usual sweet spot; fortnightly beats “whenever I have something”. Consistency trains the reader to open, and it is read as a proxy for reliability. If you can only manage monthly, commit to monthly and keep it.

5. Measure four numbers, nothing else

  • Signup rate on the main offer — is the promise strong enough?
  • Open rate on the first three emails — did they expect you?
  • Click rate — is the content leading anywhere?
  • Unsubscribe rate per send — a spike means a mismatch in expectation.

Ignore list size as a vanity figure. A 400-person list that opens every week is worth more than 4,000 that never do.

6. Choose a platform that will not punish you for growing

Before you commit to a tool, check what counts as a contact and which tier includes the automations you need — see the ten-minute guide. If you are starting from nothing and publishing regularly, a creator-first platform is usually the cheapest sensible choice; the comparison of five platforms worth shortlisting covers the trade-offs.

What not to do

  • Do not buy a list. It destroys deliverability and it is not your audience.
  • Do not import scraped addresses, ever.
  • Do not send to people who never confirmed — it is the fastest way to a spam folder.
  • Do not lead with a sales pitch before you have delivered value once.
