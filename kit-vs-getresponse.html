Kit vs GetResponse: which one fits your funnel

These two are compared constantly and the comparison usually goes wrong, because they are not competing for the same job. One is built around an audience; the other is built around a business that needs several tools in one bill.

One-line summary

  • Kit — the tool you choose when your newsletter is the business or the top of it.
  • GetResponse — the tool you choose when you want one subscription to cover email, landing pages, forms and sometimes webinars.

Where Kit wins

  • Writing-first workflow. The editor and the sequence builder are built around publishing regularly, not around assembling campaigns.
  • Creator economics. Subscriber-first pricing and a genuinely usable free tier make it the cheapest sensible way to start a list from zero.
  • Less to get wrong. Fewer switches means fewer ways to misconfigure a flow and email the wrong segment.

Where GetResponse wins

  • Breadth. Landing pages, forms, paid-ads-friendly list building and webinar tooling inside one subscription — useful if you do not want four vendors.
  • E-commerce features. Product blocks, abandoned-cart style flows and store integrations are further along than on Kit.
  • Funnel templates. If you sell via a funnel rather than a newsletter, the prebuilt structure saves setup time.

The deciding question

Ask: “Does revenue come from the list itself, or from a product behind it?”

  • From the list (newsletters, sponsorships, courses, memberships) → Kit.
  • From a product behind the list (store, services, webinars) → GetResponse.

If the honest answer is “both, and I sell physical products”, that is a vote for the e-commerce specialists rather than either of these two — see the full shortlist.

Practical differences that show up later

Automation

Both support visual automation, but they stop in different places. Kit's automations are excellent at sequence-and-condition logic around content; GetResponse goes further into time-based, multi-goal funnels. If you plan cart recovery or scored lead routing, check the tier carefully on both.

Deliverability tooling

Both support custom sending domains with SPF and DKIM. What matters is how much of the setup is guided versus documented — and how much control you get over suppression and re-engagement. Treat this as the second question you ask, after pricing.

Billing behaviour

Confirm what each one counts. Platforms differ on whether unsubscribed and long-inactive profiles stay billable. At small list sizes this is noise; at 15,000+ contacts it is the single biggest line in your costs.

Migration reality check

Subscribers move easily between these two; automations do not. Expect to rebuild every sequence and re-map every tag by hand. If you are switching, do it before you build five flows — not after.

Recommendation

Start a list from nothing, publish regularly, sell content or courses: Kit. Run a small business with a funnel and a product, and you would rather pay once: GetResponse. Either way, run the four checks in the ten-minute guide before you pay for a year.
